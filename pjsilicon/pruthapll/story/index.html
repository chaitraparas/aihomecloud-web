01 / The arc

From a dusty schematic
to sign-off.

A PLL multiplies a clean reference clock. Here it's ×8: 5–12.5 MHz in, 40–100 MHz out, built from a ring oscillator, a charge pump and a small RC filter. The job is easy to describe. Proving it works at every corner, temperature and supply voltage, with real wires and real noise, is the hard part.

2020

The internship PLL

Built during a VSD online internship. It was a working schematic, but the loop-filter capacitors alone (200 pF + 500 pF) would have taken 0.098–0.128 mm² of silicon.

2026-09-17 · 23 commits in one day

Restart, with an AI crew

The design was rebuilt on SkyWater 130 nm. 31 experiments and 4 candidate designs followed in a week. Two of them were humbling.

Sept · CANDIDATE-001

The "4.8× better" that wasn't

A headline jitter improvement turned out to be about 90% measurement-window artifact. It was caught, an erratum was written and the claim was retracted. That set the tone for everything after.

2026-10-18

The mission

The goal became the most rigorously verified, PVT-calibrated, reproducible open PLL. Every claim must climb the evidence ladder: hypothesis → analytical → transistor → nonlinear → disturbance → PVT → physical.

2026-10-20

The noise wall

Deterministic jitter was a beautiful 0.3 ps. Then real device noise was switched on, and it came out at 14.6 ps against a 10 ps budget.

04 / The noise wall

0.3 ps was a lie.
14.6 ps was the truth.

Noise-free sims make every oscillator look perfect. With thermal and flicker noise switched on in every transistor, jitter jumped 50×. The fix was more current in the ring. But more current means more power, and the noise model itself needed calibrating before the numbers could be trusted.

default noise model calibrated γ (PMOS ≈0.9, NMOS ≈2.4) 10 ps budget at 100 MHz

Period jitter vs VCO current scale k. Calibrated noise made things worse: k=6 went from 6.79 to 10.63 ps. Bias-node caps (10 pF, then 15 pF) gave the same gain as a 1.2 mW bias fix at zero extra power; with a ×1.5 VCO (p1n3) that passed every corner with a 4% worst-case margin. The layout search later settled on p1n5 with 17.1 pF caps.

05 / The sweet spot

Three walls.
One winner.

Scale the VCO up and jitter improves, but power climbs and the cold, slow corner can't reach 100 MHz. Scale it down and jitter blows the budget. A Latin-hypercube search drew 16 layouts, extracted every one, and scored each against all three constraints.

feasible jitter margin can't reach 100 MHz cold / over power winner #3
Wall 1 · jitterJitter must stay at or below 0.90× the limit. Three small, cheap rings fail here.
Wall 2 · rangeAt ss / −40 °C, bigger rings top out at 81–95 MHz, below the 100 MHz needed.
Wall 3 · powerAbove s≈1.15 the VCO alone burns more than 900 µW.

Winner #3: scale 0.96, degeneration 2376 Ω, 741 µW VCO, FoM −220.1 dB against a ≤ −217 target. The fallback is #14. #13 never ran: its bias rail missed the 0.01 µm grid.
