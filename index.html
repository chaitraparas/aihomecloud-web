Chapter 01How it works

Three moves, then it disappears into the background.

01.

Dig out the spare hardware

An old laptop, a retired desktop, or a small board costing about $35. Install the open-source server. If it can sit in a corner and stay on, it can be your cloud.

02.

Open the app. That's the setup

The Android app finds your server over home Wi-Fi automatically. No IP addresses to type, no port forwarding, no account creation — it just shows up.

03.

Backups happen on their own

Photos and files copy themselves to your machine. Search them in plain words — “ammi at the beach” — with a model that runs inside your home, not in a datacenter.

Chapter 02Never a cloud

It is not a cloud. There is no “our end.”

  • Your files never leave your hardware — not for backup, not for search, not for “service improvement.”
  • No storage subscription, ever. The core product is free because it costs us nothing to run: it runs on your machine.
  • There are no AiHomeCloud storage servers holding your life. Nothing to breach, nothing to leak, nothing to mine.
  • Your search history is as private as your photos. Both stay inside the house.
  • If AiHomeCloud the company vanished tomorrow, your setup would keep working. It's yours.
  • Cancel anything, any time — your photos don't move, because they were never here.

0bytes of your data stored on AiHomeCloud's servers

We couldn't sell, leak, or lose your files even if we wanted to. We don't have them. The machine in your hallway does.

Chapter 03Security, said two ways

Every promise, in plain words and in spec words.

The same four guarantees, twice: once for the family that just wants it to work, once for the homelab that wants to verify.

For the family — plain wordsFor the homelab — spec words
Finds itself. Open the app and your server is just there. On home Wi-Fi the phone locates it automatically — nothing to type, nothing to configure.

mDNS discovery on the LAN · service _aihomecloud._tcp.local · resolvable with dns-sd or Avahi

Trust, once. Your phone learns your server's fingerprint the first time they meet — and from then on refuses impostors. You never think about it again.

TLS 1.3 · trust-on-first-use certificate pinning · first contact seals the identity, every later connection must match it

Identity can't be faked. Even if someone broke into the server software itself, they still couldn't pretend to be your server to your phone.

certificate rotation signed by a root-owned process the network-facing API can never touch · a compromised API cannot forge server identity

Search stays home. Type “diwali sparklers” and find the photo. The understanding happens inside your house — the photo and the question never leave it.

on-device semantic embedding model · photos and queries processed locally · zero egress

Chapter 05A fair question

“Anyone can build an app with AI now. Why pay for this one?”

From the person who builds it — no script, no funnel.

A fair question, and honestly, the first version did come together fast. What that skips: the first version was maybe 20% of the real work.

AiHomeCloud took about a year, start to finish — writing the specs, designing around real families, testing on real hardware bought with my own money, and more review cycles than I'll admit. Well over $1,000 went to AI subscriptions along the way. AI sped the typing up; it didn't do the thinking, the testing, or the fixing.

And the other 80% never ends: security fixes, Android breaking something new every release, the features people actually ask for. Most solo open-source projects don't die because the code was bad — they fade quietly, when the person behind them stops feeling that anyone cares.

You never have to pay. The free tier is the complete product for one person, and it will stay that way. But if AiHomeCloud earns its place in your home, the Family unlock is a genuine way to keep it alive and improving. I'm not too proud to say it matters.

— Paras
